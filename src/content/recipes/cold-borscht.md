> [!info] Recipe is for 6 pax

### Ingredients

- 4 medium beets, about 14 ounces
- 4 Persian cucumbers
- 6 radishes
- 1/2 cup finely chopped fresh dill, plus more for garnish
- 1/2 cup finely chopped fresh parsley, plus more for garnish
- 6 cups plain kefir
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- Freshly ground black pepper
- Olive oil, for garnish
- Plain yogurt, optional topping
- Hard boiled eggs, optional topping

---

#### Directions

- Add the beets to a large saucepan and cover with water. Bring to a boil and cook for about 30 to 40 minutes until they are fork tender. Remove from the water and set aside to cool.
- Slice half of the cucumbers and radishes thinly into round coins, and slice the other half into matchsticks. Keep the coins and the matchsticks separated.
- Chop the herbs finely.
- Once the beets are cool enough to handle, peel them, then grate them on the large holes of a box grater.
- Pour the kefir into a large mixing bowl. Add the grated beets, the cucumbers and radishes that have been sliced into matchsticks, the chopped herbs, salt, and pepper. Stir until all ingredients are combined.
- Chill for 30 minutes or longer before serving. To serve, top with the thin round slices of cucumbers and radishes, more fresh dill and parsley, a dollop of yogurt, and a drizzle of olive oil. If desired, add half a hard-boiled egg on top too.

## Soup Night Notes

A tasting of this soup was done on 2025-08-08 with Annika, Peder, Katti, and Ædrian.

### My Notes and Observations

This was the soup I was more concerned about, having only tried borscht once or twice in my life and never making it before, especially not a cold version. It was an experiment, but I was pleasantly surprised by the reactions.

It is very seasonal and relies heavily on high-quality ingredients. The insanely good beets from Annika's farm made a big difference.

The flavour profile is divisive: people either love beets, dill, and that earthy brightness, or they hate it. There is no middle ground.

Visually, it is stunning. Boiled beets mixed with kefir create a rich, saturated colour that photographs beautifully. Dollops of good sour cream and an egg on top make it Instagram-worthy.

An unexpected piece of feedback: I grated the beets too coarsely, thinking it would be a problem since the soup should be looser. Instead, people wanted even more texture.

Cucumbers were excellent. Finding Persian cucumbers helped, as they were not watery. The radishes were poor quality; chopping them was unpleasant.

Ratings landed around the 90% mark. It is a stunner, a showpiece, and could become a signature dish.

### Improvements

- Split the boiled beets into two preparations:
  - Half grated extremely finely for smoothness
  - Half left in larger, al dente-style chunks for bite and contrast
- Source better radishes; consider testing daikon for better body and easier prep
- Maintain use of Persian cucumbers for texture and flavour consistency
- Fine-tune based on seasonal availability, as the soup's success depends heavily on ingredient quality
- Have a squeeze bottle for the sour cream for more accuracy when plating

### Conclusion

Cold borscht has strong potential as a signature soup, offering both visual impact and memorable flavour.

Its polarising nature means it will not be for everyone, but for those who enjoy the profile, it can deliver a striking, seasonal experience.

With tweaks to texture, radish selection, and continued use of top-tier produce, it could work as a versatile dish served either hot or cold depending on the weather.

Definitely requires good bread or a roll with garlic butter, or a garlic rye/black bread of sorts.
